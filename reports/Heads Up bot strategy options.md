# Build Short Deck PLO5 Bots From Evidence

The strongest near-term path is to test five distinct challengers against the actual latest-main Anchor baseline, while keeping a small sequence MCCFR+ policy as the higher-cost strategic experiment. **No published Hold’em result establishes strength for this repository’s 36-card heads-up Short Deck PLO5 game**, and the available league evidence supports only bounded conclusions. Latest-main Rangefinder results are positive against weak opponents, roughly even against Rook and Talon, and modestly negative against Anchor and the pressure family. The five candidates now have concrete implementations, but their evaluation depth differs: Rangeglass completed a full roster run, corrected Telltide has focused results, and the other three have smoke checks only. The decision report therefore recommends a staged, paired benchmark rather than declaring a winner in advance.

## Strong poker agents transfer architecture, not Hold’em policies

CFR provides the most relevant theoretical foundation for heads-up zero-sum imperfect-information games: minimizing counterfactual regret across information sets approaches equilibrium, with average strategy—not the current iterate—as the useful output. MCCFR samples traversals to reduce full-tree cost, and CFR+ changes regret updates to accelerate convergence on studied benchmarks. These papers support an offline solver experiment over our exact rules; they do not supply a transferable Hold’em hand chart or a proven policy for this game. ([CFR](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2007/file/08d98638c6fcd194a4b1e6992063e944-Paper.pdf), [CFR+](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5042), [MCCFR](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2009/hash/00411460f7c92d2124a67ea0f4cb5f85-Abstract.html))

DeepStack, Libratus, and Pluribus show a complementary architecture: an offline blueprint plus selective online refinement, with specialized search and substantial computation. DeepStack’s 44,000-hand professional match was heads-up no-limit Hold’em; Libratus defeated four specialists over 120,000 hands; Pluribus was six-player Hold’em and its blueprint took 12,400 CPU core-hours. Those results demonstrate engineering patterns, not feasibility under our roughly 2 ms average decision target or evidence about Omaha. ([DeepStack](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01724), [Libratus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29249696/), [Pluribus](https://noambrown.github.io/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf))

The transfer boundary matters here: this game uses a 36-card deck, five private cards, exactly two hole plus three board cards, and a custom hand-ranking order. Hold’em equity and abstractions cannot represent those rules. Every candidate must use `evaluator.omaha_score` and return a legal action; per-half state belongs in `memory`. No equilibrium or safety guarantee follows from a heuristic or reduced-game solver. The [BARGE Short Deck Omaha rules](https://www.barge.org/rulebook/short-deck-omaha.html) confirm the card-construction distinction, though that event’s betting format differs.

## Latest-main results point to pressure and range responses

At latest-main commit **7e608ed**, the active bot is **Anchor (`m5_challenger`)**. The comparison lineup includes **Talon (`m5_counterplay`), Rook (`m11_chosen`), the six City bots (Haaland, De Bruyne, Rodri, Foden, Bernardo, and Grealish), and Rangefinder**. The supplied latest-main league run puts Rangefinder at **+4.311 bb/deal versus random over three seeds**, **+0.684 versus M5 Classic**, approximately flat versus Rook and Talon, but around **−0.33 to −0.41 versus Anchor and newer pressure bots**. This suggests a useful edge against weak play that narrows against stronger policies; it does not prove which mechanism causes the losses.

Latest-main Rangefinder is the single baseline implementation, not `rangefinder_v2`: it conditions opponent samples on observed showdowns, uses **`_PRIOR_STRENGTH = 8` and `_RANGE_SAMPLES = 6`**, and applies a double prior mix. Its small sample budget and repeated prior blend make range calibration and estimator variance natural targets. Showdown-only labels are also censored: hands that do not reach showdown provide no revealed opponent holding. Anchor remains the active baseline. Talon (`m5_counterplay`) adds selective postflop folds; it has **no river bluff**. These distinctions matter when interpreting challenger deltas.

A separate method1-branch run of `m1_pressure_confirmed` beat toy zoo opponents by roughly **+7 to +12 bb/deal**, but lost to `equity_balanced` (−0.45), `equity_tight` (−1.74), `m1_reference` (−0.56), and Anchor (−0.77) across three 1,000-deal seeds. That is evidence of exploitative gains against weak policies, not broad strength. Do not merge this with latest-main results. Rangeglass has since completed a full `--zoo all --seeds 3` run against **47 opponents**, including CPU/zoo opponents, the portfolio, main research bots, all six City bots, and all five new candidates. It completed with **zero errors or fallbacks**, but still chops or loses to many strong pressure and City bots; this is a complete roster sweep, not evidence of broad superiority.

## Paired holdouts should decide which candidates survive

Use duplicate deals and seat swaps to compare each candidate directly with a frozen latest-main Anchor baseline on common seeds. Current evidence tiers differ: Rangeglass has a complete 47-opponent, three-seed roster run; corrected Telltide has a focused 1,000-deal, three-seed set against three opponents plus an interrupted roster run; Riverloom, Suitspark, and PebbleCFR have only 200-deal, one-seed smoke checks against random and Anchor. Run complete 1,000-deal duplicate halves on finalists before promotion. Report paired bb/deal differences and 95% intervals, per-opponent results, win/chop/loss, errors, and timing. Keep aggregate weighting explicit: a zoo average is not a known estimate of the future field.

Split by both seed and opponent-policy identity. Include existing zoo bots, held-out parameterized passive/calling/aggressive styles, strong baseline snapshots, and a policy that changes behavior mid-half. Preserve a final holdout while tuning thresholds. For adaptive candidates, also measure action-prediction calibration, effective sample size, adaptation frequency, and action divergence by context; improved prediction alone does not establish higher chip EV. RNR and safe-exploitation research motivates testing robustness against model error, but those guarantees do not transfer to heuristic mixtures. ([RNR](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2007/file/6e7b33fdea3adc80ebd648fffb665bb8-Paper.pdf), [Safe Exploitation Search](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b12a1d1014e952e676f5d6931d03241a-Paper-Conference.pdf))

## Conclusion

The candidates now span range estimation, opponent response EV, adaptive sampling, preflop-conditioned ranges, and sequence-level regret minimization. Rangeglass has cleared the full-roster execution check, but mixed results against pressure and City policies leave strategic value unresolved. Corrected Telltide has sound focused runtime/error checks, while the other three remain smoke-tested only; PebbleCFR’s small negative Anchor result deserves follow-up but is too noisy to conclude from. Prioritize complete paired evaluations of Rangeglass and Telltide, then expand the other three beyond smoke tests. Promote only on held-out gains against strong opponents, acceptable timing, and no sharp matchup regressions.
