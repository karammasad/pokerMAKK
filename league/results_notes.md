The frozen m1_reference contains the original bot, sampler, canonicalization, parameters, and preflop table from aa48747. The new equity_balanced, equity_tight, and equity_loose sparring policies use that frozen estimator, with (pot, half, call margin) values (0.70, 0.60, 0.02), (0.82, 0.70, 0.10), and (0.64, 0.54, -0.04). They are useful policy comparisons but are not independent equity engines or equilibrium opponents. The existing loose_passive/calling_station and maniac/always_pot pairs remain identical behaviors; nine original names represent seven distinct behaviors.

Computation changes reuse current-hand equity in memory, skip forced decisions, and evaluate our river hand once per sample batch. They preserve simulation draws and exact equity outputs. A local 120-state, cold-cache river microbenchmark in alternating order measured 34–39% less time; this is not an end-to-end speed guarantee. Cache keys intentionally assume a random opponent range; a future range-aware estimator must include its range state in its cache key.

The current policy still assumes random opponent hands and uses 32 postflop samples; it does not learn opponent ranges. The stricter thresholds are an empirically validated interim change, not a formal robustness guarantee. Relevant primary research: [Papp's poker thesis](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/papp.msc.pdf) on weighted ranges and hand potential, [restricted Nash responses](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/NIPS07-rnash.pdf), and [data-biased robust responses](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v5/johanson09a.html). Their Hold'em results do not supply Short Deck PLO5 thresholds. Further validation needs independent opponent implementations, not just new seeds of these related policies.
